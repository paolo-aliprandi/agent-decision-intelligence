A polished demo can hide an expensive mistake. Follow a simple example of what changes when you look beyond task completion.

01 / The obvious choice

96% looks better than 92%.

Two agents. The same 1,000 tasks. Agent A completes more of them successfully. A leaderboard would put it first.

So far, so simple.
02 / The inconvenient detail

Not all failures cost the same.

Agent A makes 20 critical errors. Agent B makes 2. A wrong action isn’t the same as a request for clarification. Your evaluation shouldn’t treat them equally.

Averages don’t show the whole picture.
03 / The useful answer

Choose the agent. Define the limits.

The answer isn’t “B is always better.” It’s which agent fits the task, what needs human review, and where the evidence is still too thin.

That’s a deployment decision.

03 / The approach

Your real work.
A common test.

Start with one workflow and a decision worth getting right. A focused evaluation, designed with your team—not a universal score.

01

Define what matters.

Agree on outcomes, failure costs, constraints, and what must always go to a human. Write the decision criteria before the test.

02

Test the work. And the edge cases.

Compare candidates on representative scenarios, missing information, and consequential failures. Keep tools and budgets comparable.

03

Verify, don’t just judge.

Use known outcomes, deterministic checks, and domain-expert review where appropriate. Trace findings back to the underlying cases.

04

Make the trade-offs explicit.

Compare quality, total cost, risk, and oversight. Document uncertainty and recommend where to act, review, abstain, or test further.

The principles behind the work

01 /

Independent by intent.

The aim is a decision you can defend, not a favorable rating. Evaluation assumptions and commercial relationships should be explicit.

02 /

Uncertainty included.

Sample sizes, confidence intervals, and coverage gaps belong in the result. “We don’t know yet” is a useful finding.

03 /

Humans in the model.

Autonomy isn’t all or nothing. Review, escalation, and abstention are part of a deployment policy—not afterthoughts.
